§0

Decisions of record

Settled · reopen by PR

These are closed. Anything below can change, but it changes deliberately, with a rationale — not inside a submission.

  • Format
    Single-file HTML only. No JSX, no build step.

    One self-contained .html file per entry. This makes the CI lint a single-file scan, makes “copy the source” literal, and makes the render a file:// load with nothing to compile. React via JSX is out of scope for now; an artifact that ships its own runtime inline is still just one HTML file and is fine.

  • Posture
    Showcase, not templates.

    Entries stay specific and impressive rather than genericized with placeholder content. Genericizing doubles the cost per entry for a payoff that cannot be validated before launch. The remix path is carried by prompt and customize instead. Shapes that get requested repeatedly get promoted into skills or prompt packs later — that is a separate artifact, not a change to this one.

  • Naming
    Repo awesome-claude-artifacts, site awesomeclaudeartifacts.com.

    “Claude” is used descriptively, to name what the collection is about. Every surface carries an independence disclaimer, and no Anthropic logo, wordmark, or brand color appears anywhere on the site.

  • Thumbnails
    Rendered by CI, committed by a bot.

    An Action renders thumb.webp and pushes it to the contributor's branch, so submitters never install Playwright. One committed render per entry — light theme, 800px — with dark, mobile, and full-size generated at deploy time and never committed. That keeps the repo near 5 MB at launch instead of 25 MB, while the README can still embed real thumbnails.

  • Licensing
    Tooling is MIT; every entry carries its own license.

    Contributors license only work they made themselves. A human reviews every entry for provenance and for embedded third-party content — logos, game clones, lyrics — which is the most likely takedown vector and is not automatable. A DMCA contact is published.

  • Deferred
    No monetization, no accounts, no comments.

    Instrumentation only: which prompts get copied, which matrix cells get filtered, and a one-question “what are you making?” survey on detail pages. The gallery's job in its first months is to earn attention and generate the data that would name a business later.

§1

The model

Two axes

Entries are classified on two independent axes. They answer different questions, arrive from different directions, and neither one substitutes for the other.

Use caseAxis 1 — who and why

The job the artifact does and the person doing it. This is how people arrive: they know their situation before they know what they want built.

“I'm a consultant preparing a client leave-behind.”

StyleAxis 2 — what shape

The interaction shape, independent of subject. This is how people find something to steal: the closest working example is usually from a different domain entirely.

“I need a multi-step form that produces a tailored result.”

Why style is the primary axis. A trip-budget calculator and a SaaS ROI calculator are the same artifact with different labels. Someone who needs a comparison table learns more from an excellent one built for a domain they've never worked in than from a mediocre one in their own. Use case narrows; style is what actually transfers.
§2

Use case vocabulary

Closed · 10 terms
1 required, 2 max

These are jobs, not industries. A healthcare ROI calculator is sales, with healthcare as a topic tag. Industry never becomes a use case — that is what topics is for.

teachingTeaching & learning

Built to make someone understand something, in a classroom, a course, or self-study.

e.g. Fraction manipulative · spaced-repetition drill · lab-safety walkthrough

marketingMarketing

Audience-facing collateral: attention, positioning, capture.

e.g. Launch landing page · campaign microsite · lead-gen quiz

salesSales

Used inside a deal cycle, usually one-to-one with a named prospect.

e.g. ROI calculator · product configurator · plan comparison

consultingConsulting

Client deliverables, frameworks, and assessments that replace a 40-page PDF.

e.g. Maturity model · readiness scorecard · findings report

product-designProduct & design

The work of specifying and shaping software before it is built.

e.g. Clickable prototype · journey map · now/next/later board

data-researchData & research

Making a dataset or a body of evidence legible to someone else.

e.g. CSV explorer · survey dashboard · literature map

internal-opsInternal ops & HR

Running the inside of an organisation: process, policy, people.

e.g. Org chart · benefits explainer · incident runbook

personalPersonal

One person's own life admin, with no organisation behind it.

e.g. Trip planner · budget tracker · habit log

creativeCreative work

Writing, art, and the presentation of a body of creative work.

e.g. Story bible · interactive fiction · portfolio

playPlay

Made for enjoyment. No downstream deliverable, and that is fine.

e.g. Word puzzle · generative art toy · physics sandbox

§3

Style vocabulary

Closed · 15 terms
Exactly 1

Fifteen styles, grouped into five families by how you interact rather than what they look like. Families are the filter chips; styles are the refinement. family is derived from style and never authored by hand.

Present

presentYou read it, front to back.
deck

Deck

Sequential slides with keyboard or arrow navigation. One idea per screen.

Not a report: if scrolling beats stepping, it's a report.

one-pager

One-pager

A single scroll with a persuasive rhythm, ending in an ask.

Not a report: a one-pager sells; a report informs.

report

Report

Long-form reading with real structure — sections, figures, references.

Not a dashboard: the argument is authored, not queried.

Explain

explainYou poke it to understand something.
diagram

Diagram

A spatial or structural picture. Interaction reveals, annotates, or rearranges.

Not a dashboard: the layout carries meaning, not the numbers.

timeline

Timeline

A temporal sequence you scrub, zoom, or filter.

Not a tracker: the timeline is authored and read-only.

simulator

Simulator

Parameters drive a running model. You learn by changing them and watching.

Not a calculator: a simulator has state that evolves; a calculator returns.

Calculate

calculateInputs go in, an answer comes out.
calculator

Calculator

Inputs resolve to a number or a small result set, updating live.

Not a wizard: everything is on one screen at once.

comparator

Comparator

Options side by side against shared criteria, sortable or weightable.

Not a dashboard: the rows are choices, not observations.

wizard

Wizard

A guided multi-step form that ends in a tailored output or recommendation.

Not a quiz: a wizard has no right answers, only a result.

Explore

exploreData in, many views out.
dashboard

Dashboard

A fixed dataset shown through several coordinated views and filters.

Not a data-explorer: the data ships with the artifact.

data-explorer

Data explorer

The user brings their own data — paste or upload — and the artifact makes it legible.

Not a generator: the output is a view, not a file to take away.

Engage

engageA loop, a score, or state that persists.
quiz

Quiz

Questions resolve to a score or a result type.

Not a game: a quiz ends; a game loops.

game

Game

Rules, a win or scoring condition, and a reason to play again.

Not a simulator: a game can be lost.

generator

Generator

Configure inputs, produce an output to take away — an image, text, a file.

Not a wizard: the artifact makes a thing, not a recommendation.

tracker

Tracker

You add entries over time and state survives a reload.

The only style that requires persistence. Flag it in techniques.

§5

Entry schema

artifacts/<slug>/meta.yaml

One directory per entry. Required fields are marked; everything else is optional but improves ranking and search.

# ── identity ─────────────────────────────────────────
title:        Runway & Dilution Modeler        # required
summary:      One sentence, present tense,      # required
               what it does for whom.
tier:         source                           # required · source | linked

# ── classification (§2, §3) ──────────────────────────
use_case:     [consulting, sales]              # required · 1–2, first is primary
style:        calculator                       # required · exactly 1, closed vocab
topics:       [finance, startups]              # 0–4 · curated open vocab
techniques:   [charts, localstorage]           # closed vocab · secondary nav only

# ── the thing itself ─────────────────────────────────
source:       artifact.html                    # required when tier: source · always .html
claude_url:   https://claude.ai/public/...     # required when tier: linked
thumb:        thumb.webp                       # CI writes this for tier: source

# ── the remix path — the actual product ──────────────
prompt: |                                     # strongly encouraged
  The prompt that produced this artifact, verbatim.
customize:                                     # the three things worth changing
  - Swap the funding rounds in `ROUNDS` for your own.
  - Ask Claude to add a secondary-sale scenario.
  - Replace the currency formatter for non-USD.
technical:    false                            # does remixing require reading code?

# ── provenance ───────────────────────────────────────
author:       { name: ..., github: ... }       # required
license:      MIT                              # required · OSI id or CC0
added:        2026-08-24                       # CI stamps on merge
prompt is the hero field, not a footnote. Most of the audience — marketers, teachers, consultants — will never edit the HTML. For them the prompt is the deliverable, and “Copy prompt” is the primary action on every card. Entries without one are accepted but rank below entries with one.
§6

Tagging rules

How to resolve an argument
  • Rule 1
    Exactly one style. No exceptions.

    The moment an entry can be [deck, report, one-pager], the matrix stops meaning anything and every filter returns everything. Something that genuinely reads as two styles is usually two entries — or one badly scoped entry.

  • Rule 2
    Pick style by the first ten seconds.

    Not by what it contains, but by what a person does with it immediately on load. A deck full of charts is still a deck. A report with a calculator embedded halfway down is still a report.

  • Rule 3
    Use case is the job, never the industry.

    Industry, subject matter, and domain all live in topics. If someone proposes adding fintech or edtech to the use-case vocabulary, the answer is no and the field they want is topics.

  • Rule 4
    Primary use case first in the array.

    It drives the default grouping and the “I want to…” landing sections. The second is for genuine cross-appeal only — not for reach.

  • Rule 5
    Vocabularies change by PR, not by entry.

    New terms go through a dedicated PR against taxonomy.yaml with a rationale and at least three existing entries that would move. Nobody invents a term inside a submission.

  • Rule 6
    When two styles genuinely fit, take the more specific one.

    simulator over diagram. comparator over dashboard. tracker over generator. Specific beats broad, because broad is where entries go to become unfindable.

§7

CI gates

Automate the checks,
never the decision

Every gate below blocks a PR. None of them merge it — a human reviews every entry, because the whole site is a machine for executing strangers' JavaScript in front of an audience that trusts you.

vocab

use_case, style, and techniques validate against taxonomy.yaml. Unknown term fails.

self-contained

No CDN scripts, remote stylesheets, or external images. Everything inline or a data URI.

no-network

Rejects fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket, <form action>, and <input type="password">.

render

Playwright loads the file at 1280×800, waits for fonts, screenshots light, dark, and 390px wide. The bot commits the light render.

no-console-errors

A clean load, or the entry doesn't ship. Catches broken artifacts before reviewers waste time.

weight

Single file under 2 MB. Anything larger is usually an un-optimised embedded asset.

one-file

Exactly one .html per entry. No .jsx, no bundler config, nothing to compile.

near-duplicate

Warns when style + primary use case + title similarity suggests we already have this one.

license

An OSI identifier or CC0, plus an author. Unlicensed entries cannot be remixed, which is the point.

Serve artifacts from a second origin. Gallery on the primary domain, artifacts on a separate one, embedded with sandbox="allow-scripts" and nothing else — never allow-same-origin alongside it, which defeats the sandbox entirely. Add a CSP on the artifact origin blocking connect-src and form-action. The risk being managed is not XSS on your own site; it is the gallery becoming a credible distribution channel for a convincing fake login page.
§8

The curation bar

What “awesome” means here
  • Complete
    It does the whole job, not a slice of it.

    Demos, stubs, and “imagine this worked” placeholders are declined.

  • Standalone
    It works with no network, no keys, no accounts.

    Also the security policy — the two happen to align.

  • Distinct
    Not the fortieth pomodoro timer.

    Maintain a public “we have enough of these” list: timers, to-do apps, tip splitters, unit converters. Decline with a link to the existing entry.

  • Robust
    Legible in light and dark, usable at 390px.

    Checked automatically, since the gallery shows all three renders.

  • Attributable
    A named author and a license that permits remixing.

    Remixing is the product. An entry nobody may copy is a screenshot.

§9

Build order

Static first,
backend only if earned
Phase 0

Vocabulary and validator

taxonomy.yaml, the schema, and the lint — before a single entry exists. Every PR after this is a submission instead of a taxonomy debate.

Phase 1

Seed the matrix

76 showcase entries against §4, generated deliberately across cells. Not a weekend of building whatever is fun — a weekend of filling a grid.

Phase 2

Static gallery

Masonry grid on committed WebP thumbnails, hover swaps in the live sandboxed iframe, client-side search over human phrasing (“pricing page”, “budget tracker”), detail pages with prompt, source, and customize notes. GitHub Pages.

Phase 3

One-click contribution

A GitHub issue form with vocabulary dropdowns and a code field, converted into a PR by an Action. Nobody clones a repo to submit a toy — contribution friction decides whether a hybrid model survives.

Phase 4

Stats, and nothing more

One Cloudflare Worker for view counts, anonymous likes, and a trending sort, hydrating the grid from a single optional request. If it's down the site falls back to alphabetical. No accounts, no comments — the moment there are accounts there is moderation, and this stops being a repo.
